🎮 "Who Opened That Breaker?" — Scenarios

Read first. Guess. Then reveal.

🎯 Change the LRU or Pass?

Answer YES or NO before revealing why.

GEN OFF BUS light on after start. Change IDG?

❌ NO. The GCU opened the breaker for a reason. Find the trip cause first.

External power won’t connect. Replace EPC?

❌ NO. Check BPCU and power quality — the system blocks bad power.

TR UNIT light on. Replace battery?

❌ NO. Check the TRU and its AC source. DC is downstream of AC.

Galleys lost power in flight on APU. Electrical failure?

❌ NO. Normal APU load shedding. The system is protecting itself.

Battery discharge light. Replace battery?

❌ NO. Check the charging system first — the battery is just compensating.

TRANSFER BUS OFF. Swap generator?

❌ NO. Check the upstream supply and BTB position before condemning a generator.

🛠 Top 10 ATA 24 Tribal Knowledge Rules

The stuff that saves time and parts.
The system decides power flow — everything is logic-controlled.
Never chase DC before AC — DC problems start upstream.
One bus = one source — no paralleling.
Trip = protection, not failure — understand WHY it tripped.
BPCU is the traffic controller — controls power flow and load shedding.
GCUs protect generators — if it opened, it saved the system.
Load shedding is normal — especially on APU and external power.
TRUs are silent failures — no moving parts but cause big problems.
Standby power is the last line of defense — if it’s gone, it’s serious.
Follow the path — SOURCE → BREAKER → BUS → LOAD.

🔚 Bottom Line

Sources (IDG/APU/External) feed transfer buses through controllers (BPCU/GCU/AGCU), TRUs convert AC to DC, and battery/inverter back it all up. If you understand who controls what and why protection trips happen, you'll outperform most mechanics immediately.
