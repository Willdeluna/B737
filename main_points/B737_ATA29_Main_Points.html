Big Picture — How It Works

Three hydraulic systems→System A, System B, and standby system provide normal and backup hydraulic power.
Main job = move the heavy stuff→Hydraulics are the muscle behind flight controls, gear, brakes, flaps, slats, steering, and reversers.airplane muscle
Normal pressure is about 3000 psi→Think 3000 psi as the baseline pressure when the system is healthy.
⚙️

System A vs System B

System A powers gear, steering, alternate brakes, and left reverser→A-side problems can show up as landing gear, nose wheel steering, or left T/R issues.
System B powers flaps, slats, normal brakes, and right reverser→B-side problems can look like flap/slat, normal brake, or right T/R problems.
Both systems power primary flight controls→Ailerons, elevators, and rudder get redundant hydraulic support.redundancy
🔄

Standby Hydraulic System

Backup for critical components→Standby supports rudder, leading edge devices, and thrust reversers when needed.
Can operate automatically or manually→It can come alive from system logic or crew selection.
Electric pump only→No engine-driven pump here. Standby pressure depends on the standby electric pump.backup muscle
🔧

Engine Driven Pumps — EDPs

EDP = main pressure source→It is engine driven and produces the big flow during normal operation.
Runs whenever the engine turns→The pump still rotates with the engine even if the switch removes output.
Switch OFF stops output, not rotation→Important for understanding damage risk and pump logic.big clue
High flow: about 34 GPM→EDPs have way more output than electric motor driven pumps.
⚡

Electric Motor Driven Pumps — EMDPs

Used on ground or as backup→Electric pumps let the system pressurize without engine-driven flow.
Runs on 115V AC power→Electrical power availability matters when troubleshooting pump operation.
Lower output: about 5.7 GPM→Enough for backup/ground use, but not the same muscle as an EDP.
Auto shuts off if overheated above about 255°F→Overheat can shut down the pump to protect itself.overheat logic
💡

Pump Indications

LOW PRESSURE light = pump output low→Usually indicates pressure below about 1300 psi at that pump output.
OVERHEAT light applies to electric pumps→Think high case drain temperature, not just a random light.
Low pressure is not always pump failure→Could be fluid quantity, leak, valve position, clogged filter, or indication issue.don’t parts cannon
🛢️

Reservoir System

Reservoirs store fluid and feed pumps→No fluid feed means pumps can cavitate or lose output.
Pressurized by bleed air around 42 psi→Reservoir pressure helps feed the pumps properly.
System B reservoir also feeds standby→B reservoir condition matters for standby hydraulic operation too.easy to miss
Can remain pressurized after engines off→Depressurize before opening the system.
🚧

Reservoir Safety

Depressurize before maintenance→Use the air charging valve before cracking open the system.safety first
Relief valve opens around 60–65 psi→Protects the reservoir from excessive pressure.
🔁

Power Transfer Unit — PTU

System A helps System B→PTU uses A-side pressure power to assist B-side demand.
Motor-pump design with no fluid mixing→A and B fluids stay separated while power transfers mechanically.
Mainly helps slats during B system failure→If B is weak, PTU can help leading edge devices.
Activates when B pressure drops below about 2350 psi→Simple memory: PTU = A helps B when B is weak.classic ATA 29
🧰

Hydraulic Modules & Filters

Pressure module filters, monitors, and protects→It handles filtration, pressure monitoring, and relief protection.
Relief valve opens around 3500 psi→Protects the hydraulic system from overpressure.
Three main filters: pressure, return, case drain→Filters are real-world maintenance items, not footnotes.
Return filter red indicator = clogged→Clogged filters can cause ugly hydraulic write-ups.common issue
🌡️

Cooling System

Hydraulic fluid is cooled through heat exchangers→Heat management keeps fluid and pump operation healthy.
Cooling may use fuel or air depending on system→Low fuel can increase hydraulic temperatures and trigger overheat issues.sneaky clue
🔥

EDP Supply Shutoff Valve

Closes when the fire handle is pulled→Stops hydraulic fluid flow to the engine driven pump.
Windmilling engine with valve closed can damage the pump→Know valve state before assuming the pump is safe.critical
🤖

Standby System Logic

Auto activates with low A or B pressure→Logic protects critical control when main pressure is lost.
Flaps not up and aircraft in air / above 60 knots matter→Configuration and air/ground logic help decide standby activation.
Force Fight Monitor can trigger standby rudder support→Rudder pressure disagreement can bring standby online.rudder clue
🧪

Ground Servicing

Single servicing panel fills all reservoirs→One panel supports A, B, and standby reservoir servicing.
Manual pump or pressure cart→Both methods can service the system when used correctly.
Max fill pressure is about 75 psi→Overpressure can damage components.watch the cart
📟

Indications to Know

Pressure gauges show system pressure→Use pressure indication to compare command, pump state, and system behavior.
Quantity is shown as percent→Low quantity can point toward leaks or servicing problems.
Warnings include low pressure, overheat, and low quantity→Don’t chase one light without checking the full hydraulic picture.
🔧

Maintenance / Troubleshooting Gold

Most issues are leaks, filters, or sensors→Not every hydraulic write-up needs a pump.
LOW PRESS light is not always pump failure→Check fluid, leaks, shutoff valves, electrical power, and indications.
Standby auto-activation can surprise you→Know the logic before calling it abnormal.
PTU noise can be normal→Especially when System B pressure is low and A is helping B.line mx reality
Hydraulic contamination is a big problem→Filters, fluid condition, and proper servicing matter.
