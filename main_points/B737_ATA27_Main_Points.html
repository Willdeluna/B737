Big Picture — How It All Works

Two systems: primary and secondary→Primary flies the airplane. Secondary improves performance and handling.
737 = mechanical with hydraulic boost→It is not fly-by-wire. Cables and linkages still matter.real 737 DNA
Hydraulic A and B power most controls→Redundancy keeps the aircraft controllable after failures.
↔️

Ailerons — Roll Control

Cable + hydraulic PCU system moves ailerons→Pilot input is mechanical, hydraulic power does the muscle work.
Two PCUs use A and B systems→If one side fails, the other can still assist roll control.
If one aileron jams, shear rivets isolate it→The other side can keep working.jam protection
Manual reversion is possible→Heavy controls, but still controllable.
✈️

Elevators — Pitch Control

Control columns move elevators through cables and PCUs→Pitch input travels mechanically, then gets hydraulic boost.
Autopilot and mach trim can also move elevators→Pitch complaints may involve flight control logic, not just columns.
Elevator tab function changes with flaps→Flaps up helps balance. Flaps down helps control.
🔄

Rudder — Yaw Control

Controlled by pedals and trim knob→Both pilot input and trim command affect rudder position.
Main PCU uses hydraulic A + B together→Rudder power depends on both main hydraulic systems.
Standby PCU is backup→Standby hydraulic source can preserve rudder control.
Rudder limiter reduces movement above 137 knots→Prevents overstress at higher airspeeds.speed protection
🧭

Rudder Advanced Systems

Yaw damper stabilizes aircraft automatically→Works through the flight and helps damp unwanted yaw.
Force Fight Monitor detects conflicting pressures→If A and B fight, standby rudder can be activated.high value clue
WTRIS adds rudder during manual reversion turns→Wheel-to-rudder interconnect helps roll control when hydraulics are degraded.
🪶

Spoilers & Speedbrakes

12 spoilers total→Some assist roll in flight. Others dump lift on the ground.
Spoilers move after about 11° control wheel→They help ailerons once enough roll command exists.
Speedbrake lever movement starts around 5°→Lever position matters for flight and ground logic.
Lever positions: DOWN, ARMED, FLIGHT, UP→Know the lever state before chasing spoiler write-ups.
⚠️

Auto Speedbrake Logic

Auto deploy on landing needs key conditions→Below 10 ft, gear down, thrust idle, and lever armed.testable
RTO deploys when reversers are used→Rejected takeoff logic helps dump lift and stop.
DO NOT ARM light means a system problem→Think antiskid, actuator, or signal issue.
Autostow retracts speedbrakes in protection cases→Protects the wing at high speed or weight.
Lever stop prevents full extension in some SFP cases→Prevents full extension in flight with flaps up.
🛬

Flaps — Huge Maintenance Topic

Trailing edge flaps are hydraulically powered by system B→Hydraulic B problems can become flap problems.
Alternate flaps use an electric motor backup→Slower, but gives another way to move flaps.
Detents: 0, 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 25, 30, 40→Always compare command, indication, and actual movement.
One PDU drives both wings mechanically→Torque tubes connect the entire system.
Torque brake stops the system if jammed→Protection before the drive train destroys itself.
🛑

Flap Protection Systems

Asymmetry = left vs right mismatch→System stops before the airplane gets a major rolling moment.
Skew = flap sections out of sync→Different sections not tracking together will shut the system down.
Uncommanded movement = flaps move when they shouldn’t→System shuts down to stop runaway motion.big one
All three close the bypass valve→Hydraulic power is removed to stop flap movement.
⚡

Flap Load Relief

If speed is too high, flaps retract automatically→This protects the flap structure.
Flaps 40 retract to 30 around 163 knots→Automatic movement can be normal.
Flaps 30 retract to 25 around 176 knots→Do not mistake load relief for a fault.no warning
No cockpit warning, only movement→That’s why crews may report “flaps moved by themselves.”
⚖️

Stabilizer Trim

Primary pitch trim system→Stabilizer trim changes the airplane’s pitch balance.
Electric trim is normal operation→Main method for trimming during flight.
Manual trim wheels are backup→Still available if electrical trim is not usable.
Column cutout switches stop opposite-direction trim→If pilot moves column against trim, cutout logic protects the system.runaway clue
🚧

Trim Protection

Trim cutout switches stop runaway trim→Always check cutout switches first for runaway trim write-ups.
Limit switches prevent stabilizer overtravel→Stops the system at travel limits.
Trim speed changes with flap position→Flaps up is slow. Flaps down is fast.
🎯

Feel Systems

Feel and centering units simulate aerodynamic forces→Gives the pilot realistic control resistance.
Elevator feel computer increases force with airspeed→Higher speed should feel heavier.
EFSM adds force near stall→Safety cue to discourage deeper stall entry.
🔧

Maintenance / Troubleshooting Gold

Most flight control problems are not the surfaces→Usually sensors, rigging, or control valves.
Flap issues: check asymmetry and skew first→Protection logic often tells the story.
Spoiler issues: check hydraulic shutoff valves and rigging→Bad position or shutoff logic can look like a bad spoiler.
Rudder issues: check FFM and PCU pressures→Do not ignore pressure disagreement logic.
BITE is your friend: FSEU and SMYD→Use fault data before replacing expensive parts.parts cannon stays holstered
